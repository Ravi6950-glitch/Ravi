# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

**The acquisition plan and the product are the same object.** The Product lens
says every touchpoint the buyer sees is the product, and most agencies break that
rule immediately: they sell marketing through cold outreach that is itself bad
marketing. Here the first touchpoint — GrowX Media's own content and search
presence — is a working demonstration of the thing being sold. The seam between
"how you found us" and "what we do for you" is closed by design. That is the
single best structural decision in the brief.

**One price, one invoice, one owner.** Unbundling strategy, content, ads and
account management into line items pushes the integration work onto the buyer:
they get four scopes, four approval loops and four places to blame. Bundling it
into one retainer with account management inside the bundle means the agency
absorbs the coordination. That is the right direction — own the whole chain
rather than hand the customer a kit of parts.

**The buyer and the deliverable are well matched.** A creator selling courses and
coaching lives or dies by content and paid distribution. The service is aimed at
the thing the customer already stays up at night about, not at a peripheral need
they have to be educated into caring about.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. There is no one thing.** "Full-service" is the definition of unfocused:
four disciplines, launched simultaneously, pre-launch, with no clients and a team
of unknown size. The lens asks what to cut until what remains is done
brilliantly, and nothing here has been cut. The symptom is in the price itself —
₹25,000 to ₹75,000 is a 3x spread with no named tiers and no stated difference
between the bottom and the top. A product whose price can vary threefold without
anyone being able to say what changes is not a product yet; it is a negotiation.
A buyer cannot self-select, cannot compare, and cannot tell what they are getting
for the extra ₹50,000. Fix this first, because every other problem below is
downstream of it.

**2. The experience silently depends on the customer doing the work.** The pitch
of a bundled retainer is "we handle your marketing." But for a personal brand,
the raw material is the client's own face, voice and opinions. Strategy can be
delivered without them; content for a coach cannot. So the real first thirty days
look like this: recording requests, voice calibration, revision rounds, a founder
who is already overloaded now owing the agency weekly assets. The promise says
"off your plate," the delivery says "on your plate, with homework." That gap
shows up in month two as missed deadlines on the client's side, which reads to
the client as the agency underperforming. This is the handoff that breaks, and
nothing in the brief says how it is handled — whether there is a capture process,
an on-camera substitute, or an explicit contract that the client owes raw footage
by a certain day.

**3. The first five minutes contradict the pitch if the proof is not there.**
Inbound is the only acquisition motion, and the brief explicitly flags that the
founder's own audience and track record have not been stated. Under this lens
that is not merely a funnel risk, it is an experience failure: a creator's first
five minutes with GrowX Media is landing on GrowX Media's profile and judging the
numbers on it. A marketing agency for creators, whose own creator presence is
thin, has already failed its demo before the sales conversation begins. There are
also no case studies and no client results, so there is no second source of proof
to fall back on. The product's credibility layer is currently empty and there is
no stated plan to fill it.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Say the business in one sentence, without the word "full-service," and without
  listing more than one deliverable. If you cannot, what is the one thing?
- What is the difference between the ₹25,000 client and the ₹75,000 client?
  Name it as a deliverable, not as "scope."
- Walk me through a client's day 1 to day 30. What lands in their inbox on day 1?
  What do they have to give you, and by when, for week 2 to happen?
- How much of the deliverable can be produced without the client appearing on
  camera or on a call? If the answer is "very little," you are selling their
  labour back to them.
- What is on GrowX Media's own profile today, and would a coach who found it
  decide from that page alone that you can do this? What is the plan for the
  months before inbound produces anything?
- What is the first thing a client sees that is finished and unmistakably good —
  the artefact that makes the craft visible?
- Where does the work pass between people, and who owns the client relationship
  when it does?

**Open questions this lens needs but cannot answer, and who answers them:**

- What creators actually want bundled versus bought separately, which of the four
  services is the one they would pay for alone, and whether a bundle at this
  price reads as credible or as suspiciously cheap → **/founder-consumer**
- What comparable agencies and freelancers charge creators, how they package it,
  and whether "bundled retainer" is already the category norm rather than a
  differentiator → **/founder-competitors**
- Cost to deliver one retainer, the margin at the bottom of the ₹25,000 range,
  and how many clients one operator can serve before quality drops → **/founder-cfo**
- Cost to acquire a client, and the months of runway inbound needs before it
  produces the first one → **/founder-cfo**

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Cut to one thing and lead with it.** Pick the single discipline GrowX Media
   will be known for and sell that. The other three may still be delivered, but
   they stop being the pitch. The test: the business fits in one sentence a
   creator repeats correctly to a friend.
2. **Replace the price range with two named tiers.** Each tier states what is
   produced, how often, and what the client owes in return. No undefined middle.
3. **Write the client's first 30 days as a run sheet before taking money**, with
   the client's own obligations stated as dates. Specify what happens when they
   miss one. This is the seam; it has to be designed, not improvised.
4. **Make the demo real before launch.** GrowX Media's own channel has to be the
   case study, with a stated bar (reach, inbound enquiries, or a visible body of
   work) that must be cleared before the inbound-only plan is trusted. Until it
   clears, assume a second acquisition path is needed — which the brief currently
   rules out.
5. **Confirm the one thing with buyers, not with reasoning.** Run
   /founder-consumer on the narrowed single-service pitch and the two tiers before
   committing, and /founder-competitors to check the narrowed position is not
   already occupied.

Without conditions 1 and 2, this is a PASS on the lens: a bundle with a
threefold price range and no defined centre cannot be built brilliantly, because
nobody has said what it is.
