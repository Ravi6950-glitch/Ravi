# Board verdict: GrowX Media

**Vote: 2 fund if, 1 pass. Average score 3.7 / 10.**

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 4 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

The 2–1 split is narrower than it looks. Both FUND IF votes state in writing that
they degrade to PASS if their first two conditions are not met. So all three
members agree the business **as pitched** does not stand up. They disagree only on
whether conditions can repair it: Offers and Product think yes, Monopoly thinks
the failures are structural and wants a reframed business brought back instead of
a conditional yes.

---

## Risks more than one member raised

These come first because they were found independently by different lenses.

### 1. The founder's own audience is unstated — and it is both the proof and the only channel (all three members)

Every lens landed on this, and two ranked it their single most dangerous finding.
The inbound-only plan means GrowX Media's own audience is the reason a buyer would
believe the pitch *and* the only route by which a buyer ever encounters it. Offers
called it "a single point of failure counted twice." Monopoly called the plan "the
outcome, not the method" and judged it disqualifying on its own. Product framed it
as an experience failure rather than a funnel risk: a creator's first five minutes
is landing on GrowX Media's profile and judging the numbers on it, so a marketing
agency for creators with a thin creator presence has failed its demo before any
sales conversation.

With no outbound motion planned and no case studies as a fallback, there is no
cheap test and no slow start. If the audience is near zero, nobody arrives.

**Only the founder can answer this. No skill in the pack can.**

### 2. "Full-service" is a category, not an offer — and the price range proves it (all three members)

Four disciplines launched at once, pre-launch, with nothing cut. The symptom all
three independently seized on is the price: ₹25,000–₹75,000 is a 3x spread with no
named tiers and no stated difference between floor and ceiling. Product: "a product
whose price can vary threefold without anyone being able to say what changes is not
a product yet; it is a negotiation." Offers: a published range signals negotiation
and invites the buyer to anchor at the bottom, and the double-the-price test fails
outright — nothing stated makes this obviously worth ₹150,000, which means nothing
stated makes it obviously worth ₹25,000 either. Monopoly: "creators and personal
brands" is a category, not a beachhead, so referrals never concentrate and
reputation never compounds.

### 3. The client is the product, so "we handle it" becomes homework (Offers, Product)

For a *personal* brand the raw material is the client's own face, voice, opinions
and approvals. None of it can be outsourced. Both members independently traced the
same failure: the bundle's pricing and positioning imply the buyer's effort goes to
zero, while delivery requires recording requests, voice calibration and revision
rounds from an already-overloaded founder. Product puts the break at month two,
when the client's missed deadlines read to the client as the agency
underperforming. Offers puts the cancellation in month three. Nothing in the brief
says how this handoff is managed.

### 4. Slow channel and slow results, against a retainer billed from day one (Offers, Monopoly)

Content, SEO and paid-ads learning all compound slowly — Monopoly put content-led
inbound with no audience at 12–24 months to a first qualified lead. The buyer will
not see a believable result for weeks to months while being billed monthly from day
one, which Offers calls the worst possible shape for 90-day churn. Because the
acquisition channel is slow in exactly the same window, the agency is cash-starved
at the moment its first clients are most likely to quit.

### 5. No guarantee and no second source of proof (Offers, Product, raised as a question by Monopoly)

No clients, no results, no case studies, no guarantee of any kind. The buyer is
asked to carry all the risk. Product: "the product's credibility layer is currently
empty and there is no stated plan to fill it."

### 6. No moat, and nothing that could become one (Monopoly, alone but unrebutted)

Monopoly ran four candidates and found none live: no 10x know-how; no network
effects (clients gain nothing from other clients joining); inverted economies of
scale (the eleventh retainer costs about what the first did, because it consumes
human hours); brand is the only possible candidate and does not exist yet. The
bundle, positioning and price are copyable in about a week, and client switching
cost is one month's notice. It also flagged that the ₹25k–75k band sits squeezed
between per-task freelancers and AI content tooling below, and agencies with case
studies above — with AI collapsing the cost of the content layer over time.

## Conditions, merged into one checklist

Items marked **(non-negotiable)** were flagged by their member as deciding whether
there is a business at all, rather than improving one.

- [ ] **State the founder's real audience in numbers (non-negotiable)** — platform,
      followers, monthly reach, email list, and what has personally been sold
      through it. Nothing else should be decided until this is answered.
- [ ] **Fix the channel or fix the plan (non-negotiable)** — if that audience is not
      already producing enquiries, either pair inbound with a named outbound motion
      to the first ~20 buyers, or set an explicit bar on GrowX Media's own channel
      (reach, enquiries, or a visible body of work) that must be cleared before
      inbound-only is trusted. Outbound is unusually viable here: all three members
      noted this buyer is publicly findable by name.
- [ ] **Buy the first proof rather than waiting for it (non-negotiable)** — deliver
      for 2–3 clients free or at a steeply and explicitly temporary rate, in one
      niche, in exchange for measured results and the right to publish them. Full
      price begins once two documented case studies exist.
- [ ] **Cut to one niche and one lead discipline** — the other services may still be
      delivered but stop being the pitch. Test: the business fits in one sentence a
      creator repeats correctly to a friend, without the words "full-service."
- [ ] **Replace the range with two named tiers** — each stating what is produced, how
      often, and what the client owes in return. No undefined middle, no negotiable
      band.
- [ ] **Define one guaranteed result with a timeframe** — scoped and falsifiable,
      sized against real delivery cost first.
- [ ] **Write the client's first 30 days as a run sheet before taking money** —
      including the client's own obligations as dates, and what happens when one is
      missed. This is the seam that breaks; it has to be designed, not improvised.
- [ ] **Engineer a visible week-two win** so the buyer's belief does not have to
      survive a quarter unassisted.
- [ ] **Name the offer itself** — "GrowX Media" names the company, not the thing
      being bought.
- [ ] **Validate the narrowed pitch with buyers, not with reasoning** — see the
      routed questions below.

## Open questions, routed

No member invented a figure. Each gap was named and assigned.

**Only the founder can answer:** current audience size, platform, engagement, and
personal track record. Flagged by all three; answerable by no skill.

**`/founder-consumer`** — would this buyer bundle or buy separately, and which single
service would they pay for alone? Does a bundle at this price read as credible or as
suspiciously cheap? Would they switch from doing it themselves or from a freelancer?
How many such buyers exist and can they sustain ₹25k–75k for six months?

**`/founder-competitors`** — who else sells a bundled retainer to creators, at what
price, packaged how? Is "bundled retainer" already the category norm rather than a
differentiator? What do their clients complain about publicly? Is the narrowed
position already occupied?

**`/founder-cfo`** — what does one retainer cost to deliver, including the founder's
own hours? What is the margin at the ₹25,000 floor — Offers suspects it is
unprofitable or forces delivery thin enough to produce no results. How many clients
can one operator serve before quality drops? What is the cost to acquire a client,
and how many months of runway does inbound need before producing the first one? Is
any guarantee affordable?

## The strongest version of this business the board can see

This differs from what was pitched, and the board says so plainly: it is not a
full-service agency for all creators. It is **one lead discipline, sold to one
narrow creator niche the founder knows from the inside, as one guaranteed result
with a timeframe, at two named prices.** Proof is bought up front rather than waited
for — two or three published case studies in that niche, delivered cheap in exchange
for the right to publish the numbers. Because this buyer can be identified by name
on a public feed, the first twenty clients are approached directly rather than
hoped for, while GrowX Media's own channel is built toward a stated bar that would
justify trusting inbound later. The bundle survives as *delivery*; it stops being
the *positioning*. The client's obligations are contractual and dated from day one,
so the promise and the delivery stop contradicting each other.

Monopoly's dissent stands on the record: it regards even this as earning only a
re-hearing, not a funding decision, and wants the audience number, the competitor
map and the cost to deliver in hand first. A dominant share of one small specific
market is a business it can fund. A full-service agency for all creators is not.
